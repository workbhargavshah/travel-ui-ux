# TripTide — Mini UX Case Study

## Problem
Planning a trip can become overwhelming when inspiration, places, and practical details are scattered across tabs, chats, and notes.

## Design opportunity
Create a welcoming starting point that makes planning feel lightweight. Users should quickly understand the product, discover destinations, and see a clear next step.

## Target users
- Friends organising a short group getaway
- Travellers collecting ideas before choosing dates
- People who want a simple itinerary instead of a complicated planning tool

## Primary user journey
1. Land on the TripTide homepage.
2. Scan the value proposition and destination inspiration.
3. Enter a destination, timing, and preferred travel style.
4. Review the three-step explanation of how planning works.
5. Continue toward building a trip plan.

## Design principles
- **Clarity before complexity:** Keep the first interaction short.
- **Inspiration with direction:** Use destination cards, but keep the main CTA visible.
- **Friendly and reassuring:** Warm colours and approachable language reduce the feeling of work.
- **Responsive by default:** Cards and forms stack on smaller screens.

## Success measures to test
- Can users explain TripTide's purpose after five seconds on the page?
- Can users locate the trip-planning form without help?
- Do users understand what information the form needs?
- Does the page remain readable and easy to navigate on mobile?

## Future iterations
- Test two alternative hero messages.
- Add saved destinations and collaborative trip boards.
- Conduct five usability tests and revise labels based on observed confusion.
- Add accessibility checks for contrast, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion.
